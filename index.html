The theme — health & life sciences

Health commodity tenders. Checked against clinical reality.

Medicines, devices and commodities are the largest, most error-prone lines of public health spending. One theme, mastered — depth beats breadth when the audit comes.

CHECK 01

Specs against clinical need

Item specs checked against formularies, pharmacopoeia standards and regulator registrations — so the line item is what clinicians actually prescribe, not a supplier's rebrand of last year's catalogue.

CHECK 02

Quantities against real consumption

Order sizes reconciled with consumption and stock levels at the facilities the goods are destined for — buying this year's caseload, not last year's budget line.

CHECK 03

Prices against other buyers

Unit prices benchmarked against the same commodities bought by other public buyers on the same OCDS portals — the outlier is visible before the award, not in an audit two years later.

Inside the run

Every run, the same standard. By default.

Nothing below is an enterprise upsell — it ships in every run.

LIST 01

What Quorum does

6 ITEMS
  1. 01

    Reads the whole tender. Notices, line items and awards straight from OCDS portals — no scraping, no re-keying.

  2. 02

    Runs tools that do real work. Document checklists, bidder verification, price comparison — logged MCP tools, not read-only wrappers.

  3. 03

    Borrows what is proven. Community and official MCP servers where they beat hand-rolled code — and says so in the log.

  4. 04

    Runs on open weights. Procurement data often cannot leave the country. Open-weights models by default; frontier alongside if you allow it.

  5. 05

    Logs every action. Inputs, outputs, timestamps — and a named human gate on every irreversible step.

  6. 06

    Stops before the award. The file is prepared; the decision stays with the committee. Always.

LIST 02

What you get

5 ITEMS
  1. 01

    A sourced report findings with citations down to the page — ready for the committee pack.

  2. 02

    A flag register, missing documents, lapsed registrations, price outliers — each with its evidence attached.

  3. 03

    A full audit log every tool call with inputs, outputs and timestamps. Exportable for auditors and oversight bodies.

  4. 04

    A one-page architecture brief, which tools run, which models, and exactly where your data lives.

  5. 05

    An eval harness eight-plus test tasks with pass and fail results — trust the run before you scale it.

The output

What lands in the committee pack.

One document, three panes. The report the committee reads, the flag register it rules on, and the audit log that proves how every line got there.

  • Findings written in committee language, not model output
  • Every claim carries the document, page and rows it came from
  • Flags separated from clear items — nothing buried in prose
  • The gate chip stays open until a human signs
